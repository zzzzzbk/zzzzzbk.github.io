Bat flight brings together a highly articulated skeleton and coordinated wing deformation. At Virginia Tech, I connected motion capture, skeletal animation, and machine learning to explore compact representations of maneuvering flight.

I reconstructed flight sequences in Blender and extracted kinematics across 43 bones. A variational autoencoder reduced motion dimensionality by 75%, creating a latent space for exploring motion generation and control. This work also informed bio-inspired flapping-wing design.
