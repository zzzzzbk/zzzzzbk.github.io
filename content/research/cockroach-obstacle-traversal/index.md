Inspired by insect locomotion, this study models a circular body driven toward two hinged beams while a random lateral force perturbs its path. The simplified system makes it possible to isolate the effects of propulsion, noise, and unequal obstacle stiffness.

Stronger propulsion increased traversal probability; random forcing helped only up to a point. With unequal beam stiffness, traversal favored the softer side. A Markov chain Monte Carlo model then used single-gate transition statistics to predict the body’s distribution across a larger obstacle field.

The figure above is Figure 1 from the [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09075). Published in [IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters, 7(2), 5119–5126](https://doi.org/10.1109/LRA.2022.3150831).
