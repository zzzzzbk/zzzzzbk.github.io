<p>Octopus arms are muscular hydrostats that can turn from soft and compliant to remarkably stiff on demand. I investigate the muscle-driven stiffening mechanisms behind this behavior, building high-fidelity continuum-body models (600+ DOF) that couple Cosserat rod theory with finite-element analysis to capture how muscle and passive fibrous tissue interact under load.</p>

<h2>Highlights</h2>
<ul class="detail-list">
  <li>Developed a continuum modeling framework integrating Cosserat rod theory with FEA; contributed methodology to the open-source <em>PyElastica</em>.</li>
  <li>Showed the arm actively exploits the passive response of its fibrous tissue, rather than relying on muscle activation alone.</li>
  <li>The resulting model achieves a <strong>25% reduction</strong> in control and energy effort compared to the long-held hypothesis.</li>
  <li>Analyzed muscular-hydrostat mechanics under gravitational loading to understand real-world posture and reach.</li>
</ul>
