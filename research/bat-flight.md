<p>Bats are among the most agile flyers in nature, reconfiguring a highly articulated wing many times per wingbeat. I apply deep learning and motion analysis to learn the structure of a bat's maneuvering flight from real data, and use what the model discovers to inform the design and control of bio-inspired flapping-wing drones.</p>

<h2>Highlights</h2>
<ul class="detail-list">
  <li>Built a real bat-flight dataset by extracting full skeletal kinematics (43 bones) from motion-capture data.</li>
  <li>Introduced a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) that captures the intricate nature of bat maneuvering motion, achieving <strong>75% dimensionality compression</strong>.</li>
  <li>Explored the generative latent space to enable data-driven control strategies.</li>
  <li>Advised design iterations of a bat-inspired flapping-wing drone, optimizing for a <strong>20% weight reduction</strong> as a primary constraint.</li>
</ul>
